# Pronunciation guidance review — 2026-10-18

The runtime Chinese instructions were rewritten as short, original summaries of named phonetics/teaching references. Each of the 44 symbols and all 11 recommended comparisons has `cueSourceIds` resolving to `content/pronunciation-sources.json`; the interface exposes the corresponding links under “发音依据”. References support articulation and notation facts, not endorsement of this website.

## References inspected and boundaries

- **Cambridge / UCSB IPA chart**: consonant place/manner, vowel position and lip rounding. The international chart classifies phones; it is not used to equate generic cardinal vowel audio with a British dictionary target.
- **Cambridge, The English Language, Barber, Beal and Shaw**, excerpt pp. 2–8: speech organs, voicing, English fricatives and stops, nasals, lateral/approximant articulation, and glides. The excerpt explicitly supports lower lip/upper teeth for `/f v/`, glottal friction for `/h/`, and approximant `/r/` in RP.
- **Cambridge, How Languages Work glossary**: articulatory definitions, including labiodental/interdental, stop, fricative, nasal and approximant. Indexed publisher material was readable; direct Cambridge HTML reference pages returned automated-access restrictions. They were therefore omitted from runtime source links; the reachable Cambridge English excerpt is the direct publisher reference shown instead. The Cambridge English excerpt and chart supply the substantive checks as well.
- **Oxford Learner's Dictionaries pronunciation guide**: British broad notation, optional linking `/r/`, traditional centring diphthongs, weak vowels, syllabic consonants and legitimate allophonic variation.
- **British Council, Voiced consonants**: vocal-fold vibration and clear/voiced contrasts. “Usually” is retained where natural speech can devoice sounds.
- **British Council, Schwa**: non-stressed syllables and the banana example.
- **Pronunciation Studio, Joseph Hudson**, English Vowel Sounds, Monophthongs and A Pronunciation Guide to Diphthong Vowel Sounds: jaw/tongue/lip distinctions, movement within one syllable, contemporary `/uː/` position and changes in centring diphthongs. This London teaching source uses modern transcriptions such as `/ɛ/`, `/a/`, and `/ɛː/`; the website consistently retains Oxford-style `/e/`, `/æ/`, `/eə/` and labels variation rather than silently mixing systems.

The British Council “Labial” page was **not** used as evidence for lip/teeth orientation: its indexed wording refers to the top lip, which conflicts with standard labiodental articulation. Cambridge independently confirms **lower lip and upper teeth**, which is the Chinese instruction used here. Credibility does not remove the need to cross-check a specific statement.

## Checks applied to every cue

- Match the intended place and manner of articulation; separate nasality from mouth blockage and voicing from aspiration.
- Treat vowels as tongue/lip/jaw configurations, rather than duration alone. In `/e æ aɪ/`, the first two are monophthongs; `/æ/` is more open, while `/aɪ/` has a glide toward `/ɪ/` within one syllable.
- Do not prescribe Chinese characters/pinyin as exact substitutes.
- Use the non-rhotic British dictionary convention for the displayed forms. English `/r/` is described as an approximant in this model, not the IPA trill `[r]`.
- Keep traditional `/ɪə eə ʊə/` variants explicit; there is no claim that every British speaker produces a diphthong in these words.
- Keep tips concise; fuller rationale stays behind the source disclosure.

## Specific correction

The former `/v/` instruction referred to the “first consonant” of *television*, which is `/t/`, not `/v/`. The revised cue describes the labiodental voiced articulation directly; the word transcription highlights medial `/v/`. This avoids directing a beginner to the wrong part of the recording.

## Example-word policy

Every symbol now has six distinct example words (264 symbol–word entries, 177 distinct words), with original short Chinese glosses and broad British transcriptions. The first word preserves the existing one-word tile preview and recorded example. Additional words link to a Cambridge pronunciation lookup; these are reference links, not a claim that Cambridge supplied local audio or endorsed the selection. Monosyllabic familiar words are preferred. Less frequent examples are necessary for `/ʒ/` and traditional `/ʊə/`; they are not advertised as an A1 list.

The 37 existing licensed human recordings are unchanged and remain available for every symbol. Five additional word examples per symbol do not acquire invented, synthesized or silently substituted recordings. The interface distinguishes the existing local playback button from dictionary lookup links on the six words. No publisher definitions, diagrams or recordings were copied.

This is a source-based editorial check of Chinese explanations and broad conventions. It is not a claim of an independent expert audit of every recording or a physical learner study.
